Request for criticism

Proof-Carrying Audit Warrants

A small demonstrator for making the warrant of an audit claim independently checkable — deliberately finished rather than extended. I am looking for people to tell me the distinctions it enforces are wrong, already handled, or not worth the overhead.

Why I think this is worth your time

I built a small system for making the warrant of an audit claim independently checkable. The interesting result was that the checks repeatedly caught problems in the project itself.

  • —A preservation theorem I had specified turned out to be false. A warrant resting on no admitted evidence is not preserved under extension; the countermodel is in the repository.
  • —Three BenchRisk source mappings each contained a distinction the planned formalisation would otherwise have missed.
  • —The extraction gate caught an architectural representation that could not produce type-correct OCaml, forcing a bounded refactor.
  • —The final closure review found claims in my own documentation that had ceased to be true.

None of these failures prevented the software from running. They were found because the project required each claim to carry an inspectable warrant. That is the thesis, demonstrated on itself rather than asserted.

The proposition

A mitigation's effect and a mitigation's warrant are separate audit objects. The effect may remain unchanged while the warrant expires, is defeated, or becomes inapplicable.

A risk framework can tell you what follows if a mitigation is present. It is a separate question what entitles you to say it is present, still applies, and still holds.

The requirement is not mine. Frontier AI Auditing (arXiv:2601.11699v4) states it: audit findings “should carry explicit assumptions and validity conditions, and should be automatically deprecated when key underlying assumptions no longer hold.” What I have built is a mechanism for that requirement, not the requirement.

Four verdicts, which are logical categories rather than a confidence scale:

WARRANTEDadmitted evidence meets the obligations, under declared conditions
DEFEATEDadmitted evidence establishes that a required condition fails
UNDERDETERMINEDthe evidence decides neither way
INADMISSIBLEthis evidence may not participate in deciding this claim

The three source findings

Each case began by reading the complete BenchRisk registry row — every field, all sibling mitigations — before writing any proof. Three cases, three findings, none anticipated.

The elicitation instrument is weaker than the measure it elicits

Mitigation 67 · registry v0.5.2

The same registry row states the response measure as “Do not allow evaluation on demand” and the assessment question as “Do you restrict or avoid evaluation on demand…?” A benchmark author can answer that question honestly while operating a restricted-but-permitted regime the response measure excludes. Both strings are in one row, so this is not version drift.

answer to an assessment instrument≠warrant for the mitigation statement

A mitigation is not the negation of its failure mode

Mitigation 93 · failure mode #025

M-93 asserts the evaluator is algorithmic and that no legitimate reason exists to embed it in the system chain. It does not assert the evaluator is absent from the chain. So M-93 can hold while FM-025 has occurred — consistent with its being scored as a likelihood mitigation rather than a logical exclusion.

mitigation warrant≠warrant that the failure mode is absent

Declaring a scope is not covering it

Mitigation 162 · failure mode #002

M-162 requires a complete definition of what is and is not covered. BenchRisk itself separates this from achieved coverage, giving FM-002 distinct mitigations for measurement (M-68, M-190). If declaration were achievement, those would be redundant.

declared coverage≠adequate coverage

These are observations from three selected mappings, not claims that BenchRisk as a whole is defective or internally inconsistent. BenchRisk documents its own evidence boundary plainly, which is why it can be cited exactly.

One executable example

$ warrant-check examples/structural-s1.json
VERDICT
UNDERDETERMINED

CLAIM
The evaluator applies an algorithm to a list of correct
answers, and there is no legitimate reason to incorporate
it into the system chain

MISSING OBLIGATIONS
  NoLegitimateReasonToEmbed

INTERPRETATION
The admitted evidence decides neither way. This is not
evidence that the claim is false. Obtain the missing
evidence listed above, or weaken the claim.

The evidence here says the evaluator is an algorithmic lookup and is outside the system chain. A naive checker reports pass. This one reports that one of the two propositions M-93 actually asserts has not been established, and names which — because “no legitimate reason exists” is a judgement no architectural fact settles.

What this deliberately does not claim

  • It does not establish that an AI system is safe, or that supplied evidence is true.
  • WARRANTED is not permanent. DEFEATED does not allege the earlier audit was mistaken. UNDERDETERMINED is not evidence the claim is false. INADMISSIBLE does not mean the model cannot perform the task.
  • A change signal is not a defeat. DEFEATED requires admitted evidence establishing failure of a declared condition; an unexplained change belongs in UNDERDETERMINED.
  • It does not establish completeness. It reasons over the ledger it is given. WARRANTED over a curated ledger is worth what the curation is worth.
  • Coq extraction, the OCaml compiler, and the JSON parser are not verified. What is established is observed agreement between the executable and the formal decisions on all 14 published fixtures. That is testing, not proof.
  • Retraction is out of scope: the ledger is monotone, so defeat arrives only by addition.

Five questions

I am asking you to tell me I am wrong. Any of these being answered “no” is useful information.

01

Does your process currently distinguish excluded evidence from insufficient evidence?

They direct different remediation — change the claim or the evidence source, versus go and get the missing evidence. Is that distinction preserved in your findings today, or does it collapse?

02

Is the warrant boundary drawn in the right place?

The sharpest case: I treat “no legitimate reason to embed the evaluator” as something structural evidence can never discharge. That is my modelling decision, not BenchRisk's. Do you disagree — and if so, does the system at least make the disagreement precise enough to inspect?

03

Does the M-93 result matter operationally?

A mitigation can be satisfied while the failure mode it mitigates has occurred. Is that a distinction your assessment machinery already preserves, or one it loses?

04

Is declared-versus-adequate coverage already handled where you work?

Or is “the benchmark says it covers X” routinely read as “the benchmark covers X”?

05

Would carrying this object with a finding improve real audit work?

claim            subject           admitted evidence
assumptions      scope             validity conditions
defeat conditions                  current verdict
witness / reason

Is that more useful than a conventional conclusion whose authority conditions live in prose — or is it overhead that would not survive contact with a real engagement?

What I am not asking

Whether the proof assistant is impressive. The formal development exists so the decision logic can be checked independently and cheaply; it is not the contribution I am asking you to evaluate.

If the answer is that this solves no problem you have, that is a result, and I would rather have it now than after building more.
